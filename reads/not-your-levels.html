Not your levels,
your sensitivity

3 min read · checked against its sources

Many people with PMDD have their hormones tested, and the results come back normal. That does not mean nothing is wrong. It is what the research would predict.

Normal hormones, a different response

People with PMDD have the same hormone levels, and the same monthly rise and fall, as everyone else. The difference is in how the brain responds to those normal changes. After ovulation, that response can turn into a storm in mood, and it settles again once the period starts.

In a study at the US National Institutes of Health, women with premenstrual symptoms were given a medicine that paused their own hormones for a while, and their symptoms went quiet. When estrogen or progesterone was added back, the symptoms returned. Women without premenstrual symptoms had no such reaction to the same hormones.

A difference in the cells

Later work from the same institute found that cells from women with PMDD handle these hormones differently, right down to the genes involved. The research is still going, but it points to something built into the body, not a weakness of character.

Why it matters

It explains why no blood test can show PMDD, and why a daily record matters so much. It also means there is no level to correct. The options doctors discuss either calm the reaction or smooth out the rise and fall that sets it off.

None of this is your fault, and none of it is imagined.
