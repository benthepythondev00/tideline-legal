Birth control
and your mood

3 min read · checked against its sources

Hormonal contraception affects mood differently from person to person. Some people feel steadier on it, and some feel worse.

Why some feel better

Many hormonal methods can stop ovulation. Without ovulation there is no monthly rise and fall to react to, which is why certain combined pills are one of the options doctors use for PMDD.

Why some feel worse

The progestogens in some methods can bring mood symptoms of their own. That is a real effect, and it is different from PMDD.

What it means for your record

PMDD should not be diagnosed while you use a hormonal method that stops ovulation, because the cycle it depends on is not there. If you use the pill, the patch, the ring, the implant, the injection or a hormonal coil, tell your doctor when you share your record.

Before you change anything

Talk to your doctor before starting, stopping or switching. Plan how you will stay protected in the meantime, and keep tracking for a few months after any change so you can see what it did.
