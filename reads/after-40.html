PMDD after 40
and perimenopause

3 min read · checked against its sources

In the years before periods stop, some people find their premenstrual weeks getting harder, or notice them for the first time. This stretch is called perimenopause, and it is different for everyone.

Why it can change

PMDD can get worse around big hormonal changes in life, such as pregnancy, giving birth and menopause. Perimenopause is one of them. Periods often change first: they may come more or less often, and get heavier or lighter.

For a brain that reacts to the monthly rise and fall, a less regular cycle can make the hard weeks harder to predict.

Telling them apart

Perimenopause can bring mood swings, low mood and brain fog of its own, on any day of the cycle, and poor sleep makes them worse. What still points to your cycle is timing: worse before a period, easing after it starts. A daily record shows that, even when the dates get less regular.

Worth raising early

If your hard weeks are changing in your forties, tell your doctor what has changed and when, and bring the record. Perimenopause is common and there is help for it, so it is a conversation worth having sooner rather than later.
