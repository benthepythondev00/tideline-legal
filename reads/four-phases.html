Your cycle,
in four parts

3 min read · checked against its sources

Your period

Day one is the first day of bleeding. Hormone levels are at their lowest. Many people feel tired and crampy at first, and lighter as the days go on.

The follicular phase

After your period, estrogen rises as your body prepares an egg. Energy and mood often lift. This is the stretch many people describe as feeling most like themselves.

Ovulation

Around the middle of the cycle an egg is released. This is the fertile window. Some people notice a twinge on one side, clearer discharge, or a higher sex drive.

The luteal phase

After ovulation, progesterone rises, and then both hormones fall away if there is no pregnancy. For people with PMS or PMDD this is when symptoms arrive, usually in the last week.

The lengths vary

Textbooks draw a 28 day cycle. Real ones run anywhere from about three to five weeks, and the first half is the part that stretches. That is why Tideline shows a window for your next period, not a single date.
