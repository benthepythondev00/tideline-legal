What to look for
in a PMDD tracker

By Ben Jonas Goedeke · 2 min read · checked against its sources · updated 10 Oct 2026

A tracker for PMDD has one job: a daily record that a doctor can read. Before you choose one, check five things.

It asks every day, on the day

The assessment rests on notes written as you go, for at least two cycles. An app that only asks about your period, or lets you fill in last week from memory, will not give you that.

It records how strong, not only what

"Irritable" on a mild day and on a day you could not go to work are different. Look for a rating, and a way to say how much the day was disrupted. That is what the DRSP, the best-studied daily chart, asks for.

It lines your days up with your cycle

What matters is whether symptoms rise in the week before your period and ease after it starts. The app should show that comparison, not just a calendar of entries.

It is quick on the worst day

The hard week is when a long form gets skipped. The fewer taps on a bad day, the more complete two cycles will be.

You know where your entries go

Check whether it needs an account, whether entries are stored on the company's servers, and whether you can export everything as a file.
